surround_sound How Cardioid Subwoofer Cancellation Cleans Up Live Stages

Because low frequencies (30 Hz to 100 Hz) have long acoustic wavelengths (spanning 3 to 11 meters), individual subwoofer enclosures radiate sound omnidirectionally—sending just as much low-end energy backward onto the stage as forward into the crowd. This stage bleed washes out vocal clarity, triggers low-frequency feedback through open stage mics, and overwhelms musicians on stage.

Acoustic Displacement & Electronic Delay Summation

In an End-Fire Array or Inverted Cardioid Stack, subwoofers are positioned with deliberate physical front-to-back spacing (typically one-quarter wavelength of the target frequency) and delayed electronically. Moving toward the audience, the physical travel time and electronic delay sync up perfectly, producing powerful forward summation (+3 to +6 dB). Toward the rear stage, the arrivals meet 180 degrees out of phase, creating deep cancellation (-15 to -25 dB rejection) and leaving the stage quiet and punchy.

Accurate physical placement is critical: even a few inches of placement error on stage will shift the phase cancellation notch, degrading rear rejection and spilling muddy bass back onto your performers.

warning Real-World Venue Realities & Manufacturer Presets

While mathematical calculations provide the essential spatial baseline, real-world cardioid arrays are affected by enclosure acoustics:

  • Cabinet Depth & Port Phase Shifts: Bass-reflex ports exhibit steep phase wrap around their tuning frequency, meaning sound does not emerge instantaneously from the front baffle.
  • Manufacturer Calibration: Major touring brands (d&b audiotechnik, L-Acoustics, Meyer Sound) utilize proprietary all-pass filters in their factory presets to linearize phase. help Frequently Asked Questions

How does a broadside horizontal sub array eliminate 'power alley' comb filtering?
When subwoofers are split into left and right stage stacks, their sound waves collide and create alternating comb-filtering nulls and loud zones (the 'power alley') across the venue. Arranging subwoofers in a continuous horizontal broadside array across the stage lip spaced under half a wavelength creates a single coherent line source, delivering smooth, uniform bass across the entire audience.
At what frequency should I tune my cardioid rear cancellation?
Most touring sound engineers tune cardioid cancellation between 63 Hz and 80 Hz. This frequency zone is where sub-bass bleed most aggressively contaminates open vocal microphones, drum vocal spill, and acoustic stage instruments.
Why do manufacturer presets require physical cabinet spacing verification?
Factory DSP presets (from d&b, L-Acoustics, Meyer Sound) depend on precise physical geometry. Even though internal digital all-pass filters handle phase alignment, cabinets must still be positioned at exact physical distances for destructive acoustic interference to cancel sound off the stage.
